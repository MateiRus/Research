## 1. Private healthcare market structure and size 2024–2026; digital services of the big networks; build vs buy

### Takeaway
The private market is consolidated around three groups. MedLife reported about €630m pro-forma revenue in 2025, Medicover Romania (with Synevo) over 1.6bn lei in 2025, and Regina Maria (sold to Finland's Mehiläinen, closed around Dec 2025) over 2bn lei already in 2023. Imaging (Affidea/Hiperdia, Medima) and labs (Synevo, Bioclinica, MedLife labs) are separate scaled verticals. The big networks run their own apps, telemedicine and online booking. Medicover even has a Romanian tech subsidiary. That makes them poor early customers for a solo founder's software, while the long tail of small clinics is underserved but has small budgets **[Plausible]**.

### Inferences
- The top three (MedLife about 3.17bn lei, Medicover about 1.6bn lei, Regina Maria above 2bn lei in 2023) total roughly 7bn lei or more. That is consistent with a total private market well above the old €570m figure. If the 15bn lei figure is right, the top three hold roughly half of it **[Plausible]**.
- Private health insurance (about 1.2–1.5bn lei a year) is small next to provider revenues. Most private spending is out-of-pocket or employer subscriptions, not insurance. So insurers are unlikely to be the main early buyer of prevention software **[Plausible]**.
- The big three are buyers with in-house IT (Medicover Tech, MedLife's AI/genomics push), long procurement cycles and international group standards (Mehiläinen, Medicover AB). For a solo founder they are more realistic as later partners or acquirers than as first customers **[Plausible]**.
- The Regina Maria ownership change may trigger IT harmonisation with Mehiläinen's Nordic digital stack. This could close doors to local vendors, or open some for integration and migration work **[Speculative]**.

### Gaps
- No 2025 revenue found for Regina Maria, Sanador, Affidea Romania, Synevo (standalone, beyond about 600m lei), Bioclinica, Medsana, Ponderas, Arcadia (Iași) or Clinica Polaris (Cluj). Their digital services were not researched because the search budget ran out.
- No authoritative 2025/2026 total market-size figure (PwC/KPMG/ZF study) was confirmed.
- I found no evidence on whether MedLife and Regina Maria build or buy their patient apps, LIS or HIS. Only the existence of Medicover Tech SRL was found.
- Insurer prevention programmes (Signal Iduna, Allianz-Țiriac, NN, Groupama, Generali, Uniqa) were not researched.

## 2. Software vendors selling to Romanian clinics, labs, pharmacies and occupational-health providers; APIs; pricing

### Takeaway
The Romanian clinic-software market is fragmented and mostly local. CNAS gives away free SIUI reporting apps. Small SaaS practice-management tools such as MediNote (50 lei/user/month) and BizMedica/Setrio (159–199 RON/month, older price) bundle booking, patient files and CNAS reporting. Hospitals rely on a few local HIS vendors: Hipocrate/RSC, InfoWorld and Softeh, the first of which suffered a 26-hospital ransomware outage in 2024. Booking is led by the Docbook marketplace, and a wave of 2025–2026 AI-receptionist startups targets clinics. **No vendor with a documented public API was found.** "Docbook" and "Zarina CRM" exist. "Softeh" exists as a hospital-IS vendor. "Hipocrate" is a real product by RSC (not InfoWorld). "Atlas", "Medsoft", CompuGroup and Asseco could not be verified **[Established for the named vendors; APIs unknown]**.

### Inferences
- The free CNAS SIUI apps cap what small CNAS-contracted practices will pay for "reporting only" software. Commercial value has to come from booking, reminders, patient files and invoicing bundled together, which is what MediNote and BizMedica sell **[Plausible]**.
- Price anchors: about 50 lei/user/month (MediNote) and 159–199 RON/month per practice (BizMedica, older). A prevention or recall add-on for small clinics would likely need to price in the tens of euros per month per site unless it shows direct revenue recovery **[Plausible]**.
- With no visible public APIs among local PMS vendors, an integration-light product is more realistic: CSV export/import, an e-mail/SMS/WhatsApp layer, or an APEX-based overlay. Deep integrations would have to be negotiated vendor by vendor **[Plausible]**. The apparent "API gap" may reflect weak competition (small vendors), low demand (clinics rarely ask), or simply missing information (APIs exist but are not publicised). It is not evidence of an opportunity on its own.
- Hospital HIS is concentrated among a few local vendors and bought through public procurement (SEAP), with security weaknesses exposed in 2024. That makes it a hard, slow market for a solo founder **[Plausible]**.

### Gaps
- LIS vendors (labs), RIS/PACS vendors (beyond the international OEMs implied by the GE equipment at Medima), pharmacy software, and occupational-health software: **none identified in this session**.
- I could not verify whether **CompuGroup Medical, Asseco, "Atlas", "Medsoft" or "Softeh"** (beyond the 2016 survey) currently sell in Romania, or what they sell.
- No public API documentation was found for MediNote, BizMedica, iStoma, icMED, iClinic, Zarina or Docbook.
- No formal CNAS software certification or accreditation regime was confirmed. Only interface specifications plus certificate requirements were found (see Q4).

## 3. Administrative and patient-data processes that remain manual in Romanian private clinics

### Takeaway
Evidence points to the **phone as the dominant booking channel**. In vendor data from 100k+ calls, about 40% of new-appointment callers leave the first call without a booking. Online booking or record access reached only about 10% of the population in 2024. Hospitals see their EHRs as closed and non-functional. GDPR fines show patient data moving over WhatsApp, e-mail and personal hard disks. I found no direct Romanian data on paper consent forms, result delivery, recall, or follow-up after abnormal results. Those process gaps are **[Plausible]** but **not measured**.

### Cited Findings
- **[prior note]** MedOcean analysed 100,000+ calls between prospective patients and private clinics (Jan 2025–Jan 2026). 39.6% of callers seeking a new appointment ended the first call without one. The booking rate was 83.77% with empathetic operators against 21.88% with cold ones, and 5.84% when no clear slot was proposed. This is vendor data with a conflict of interest **[Strong emerging]** — [AGERPRES](https://agerpres.ro/comunicate/2026/01/30/comunicat-de-presa---medocean--1523680); [Economica.net](https://www.economica.net/foto-studiu-in-premiera-in-sistemul-medical-de-ce-pierd-clinicile-4-din-10-pacienti-romani-de-la-primul-apel_908536.html)
- **[prior note]** Ipsos (July 2021, urban 18–60): for remote consultations, 20% used the phone and 12% WhatsApp. Romanian telemedicine was described as phone/WhatsApp-dominated (dated) — [Ipsos Romania](https://www.ipsos.com/ro-ro/retrospectiva-privind-accesarea-serviciilor-medicale-pandemie)
- About **10%** of Romanians booked medical appointments online or accessed their health records in 2024. People with higher education were 10× more likely to book online **[Established]** — [OECD Reviews of Health Systems: Romania 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-reviews-of-health-systems-romania-2025_f52e4a98-en/full-report/access-and-quality-of-care-in-romania-s-healthcare-system_31f62789.html); [State of Health in the EU – Romania 2025](https://eurohealthobservatory.who.int/docs/librariesprovider3/country-health-profiles/chp2025pdf/soheu-2025-romania-final-web.pdf?download=true)

- **[prior note]** Legal constraints on automating recall and outreach:

  - **Law 190/2018 art. 3(1)**: processing health data for automated decision-making or profiling requires explicit consent or an express legal basis.
  - **Law 46/2003** (patient rights) on confidentiality.
  - Sources: [Avocatnet](https://www.avocatnet.ro/articol_15269/Orange-amendata-cu-5000-lei-pentru-mesaje-comerciale-nesolicitate.html); [Rubinian – Law 190/2018](https://www.rubinian.com/legea-190-2018-masuri-de-punere-in-aplicare-a-regulamentului-ue-2016-679-gdpr); [Law 46/2003 PDF](https://www.spitalspiridon.ro/docs/2024/Legislatie/LEGEA%20drepturilor%20pacientului%20nr.%2046%20din%2021%20ianuarie%202003.pdf)
- **[prior note]** For dental clinics, an earlier project note proposed a "recovered unscheduled treatment plans" service on the premise that clinics do not systematically recall patients. That premise was supported only by US data (Henry Schein One, Jarvis Analytics), not Romanian data — [Henry Schein One](https://www.henryscheinone.com/insights/blogs/average-providers-are-losing-hundreds-of-thousands-in-unscheduled-treatment/)

### Inferences
- Manual and phone booking, plus WhatsApp/e-mail result delivery, are widespread enough to generate GDPR enforcement. This suggests demand for compliant patient-messaging, result-delivery and recall workflows **[Plausible]**. Whether clinics will pay is unproven. Alternative explanations: low patient demand for digital channels (31.8% basic digital skills), reimbursement that does not pay for follow-up, and PMS vendors already bundling SMS reminders (MediNote).
- Follow-up after abnormal results is the closest fit to "predictive/preventive" workflow. But no Romanian evidence was found that clinics lose patients there, and CNAS does not reimburse care-coordination time. Any such product would need a revenue argument: follow-up visits and tests the clinic bills **[Speculative]**.
- Profiling or risk-scoring patients from health data triggers Law 190/2018 art. 3 explicit consent. A "predictive" tool is legally easier as clinician-facing decision support, with the clinic as controller and the vendor as processor, than as autonomous patient profiling **[Plausible]**.

### Gaps
- No Romanian survey found on: paper vs digital informed-consent forms, result-delivery channels, recall rates, follow-up of abnormal lab results, or no-show rates.
- No data on how labs (Synevo, Bioclinica, MedLife) notify critical or abnormal values to patients or referring doctors.

## 5. Prevention market and payers: CNAS prevention, screening programmes, occupational health, employer subscriptions, insurers, wellness

### Takeaway
Public prevention money exists but flows through **family doctors**, through CNAS prevention packages (since 2026 open to the uninsured if they are on a GP's list), and through **EU-funded regional screening projects** (Programul Sănătate 2021–2027). These projects run FIT/HPV/mammography screening with strict invitation, follow-up and colonoscopy referral workflows, for example ROCCAS 4 Nord-Vest, which includes Bihor, 2025–2029. The private prevention market is driven by **employer subscriptions** (400 EUR/yr tax-advantaged) and **mandatory occupational medicine**, priced at about 80–110 lei per employee per year in Bucharest. Neither market's size nor its software stack was found **[Established for the rules and programmes; market sizes unknown]**.

### Inferences
- **Screening projects are the clearest existing "prevention workflow" buyer.** They must invite, track, recall FIT-positives (about 6.7%) to colonoscopy, and report indicators to managing authorities. They are, however, run by consortia of public hospitals, universities and NGOs (e.g. ARPS) under EU procurement rules and fixed budgets set in 2025. Selling into them as a solo founder is plausible only as a subcontractor to an existing consortium partner **[Plausible]**.
- **Occupational medicine** is a recurring, legally mandated, schedule-driven process: periodic exams every 6–12 months per employee, fitness certificates, due-date tracking. Its prices are low (about 80 lei per employee per year), so providers are volume businesses that would value automation. But small margins also limit software budgets. Market size and incumbent software are unknown, so the gap could reflect weak competition or low willingness to pay **[Plausible]**.
- **Employer subscriptions** are the commercial engine of "prevention packages" at the big networks. The €400 cap is a fixed, old ceiling (unchanged since 2016), which constrains per-employee spend **[Plausible]**.
- Low primary-care spending (10%) and GP-gated prevention mean prevention data (risk assessments, test results) sits mostly in family-doctor software and CNAS systems, not in private clinics. That makes data access a structural barrier for "predictive" software outside GP practices **[Plausible]**.

## 8. Digital adoption indicators (Eurostat, Digital Decade eHealth, online access to health records)

### Takeaway
Romania is a laggard that is improving fast:
- Digital Decade eHealth score about **75 vs EU 83** in 2025, +17 points since 2023, one of the largest gains
- only **~10%** of people booked appointments online or accessed records in 2024
- **8%** accessed records online in 2022, the 2nd lowest in the EU
- the **lowest basic digital skills in the EU (31.8%, 2025)**
- Romanian firms last in AI adoption (5.2%)

The Sept 2026 national portal should lift record-access indicators, but uptake is uncertain **[Established]**.

### Cited Findings
- Digital Decade 2025 eHealth indicator: **Romania 75%** (EU-27 83%, +4 pp vs 2023). ### Inferences
- The patient side is the binding constraint.
