## Q1. Which CEE/Nordic healthtech businesses reached real recurring revenue, and with what customer (private clinics, labs, insurers, employers, public payers)?

### Takeaway
The CEE companies with verifiable recurring revenue sell either **to private doctors and clinics** (Docplanner's SaaS on top of a free patient marketplace, which reached EBITDA profitability in 2025) or **to device and diagnostics partners and payers abroad** (Medicalgorithmics: PLN 31M in 2025, with the US as its growth engine). The Nordic digital-health platforms earn recurring revenue mainly from **public regional payers** (Swedish regions buying Visiba, Platform24 and Doctrin, or reimbursing Kry visits). Germany's DiGA shows a statutory-insurer per-prescription model with average prices of about €645 (2023). Generalist IT groups (Comarch) found healthcare to be a small, shrinking line and divested it. Revenue figures for most venture-backed CEE healthtechs (Infermedica, Cardiomatics) are **not public**.

### Inferences
- **[Strong emerging]** In CEE, the **private doctor or clinic is the most reliable recurring SaaS payer**. Docplanner monetises doctors and keeps patients free, Docbook charges per booking, and MedOcean and receptie-clinica.ai sell to clinics. In the Nordics, the reliable payer is the **public region**, which buys platforms (Visiba, Platform24) or reimburses visits (Kry).
- **[Strong emerging]** Medicalgorithmics' recovery came from **selling technology into a reimbursed foreign market (US)**, not from Polish public payers: US revenue grew +140% while the total grew +29%. A Romanian vendor with a regulated diagnostic product would face the same logic: Romania's domestic payer pool is too small and slow to fund it.
- **[Strong emerging]** Comarch's exit from HIS and clinic operations, with health revenue down 40% in 2024, suggests that **broad hospital IT sold through public tenders in CEE is low-growth and low-margin** even for a large incumbent. A solo founder should not enter that segment.
- **[Plausible]** Revenue scale reference points: a narrow, well-executed B2B health SaaS in the Nordics (Kaiku) was an exit-able asset at about **€1.3M revenue**. A listed CEE medtech (Medicalgorithmics) runs at about **€7M**. Docplanner-scale (≥$100M) required €100M+ of venture funding.
- **[Plausible]** DiGA-style per-prescription reimbursement (~€645) shows what a **payer-backed digital therapeutic** can earn, but it requires an RCT and MDR certification. No equivalent pathway exists in Romania (see Q4 Gaps).

- No 2024/2025 revenue for Infermedica, Cardiomatics, Doctrin, Visiba, Platform24 or Tandem (ARR). No Kry 2025 group accounts.

## Q2. Which international players already sell into Romania (Docplanner? Medicover? Asseco? Comarch? CompuGroup Medical? others) and what do they sell?

### Takeaway
Of the named players, only **Medicover** is clearly and heavily present in Romania, and it sells **healthcare services (clinics, hospital, Synevo labs)**, not software. **Docplanner has no current Romanian operation.** A ~2016 docplanner.ro was a listing and review directory with no booking and no local team, and today's group lists 13 countries without Romania. No evidence was found of current **Asseco, Comarch or CompuGroup Medical** healthcare software deployments in Romania (CGM targeted Romania via a Vienna-based CEE unit in 2007). The Romanian online-booking niche is held by local players (Docbook, Heymedica, the big private networks' own portals) and, from Q4 2026, by the state's e-SănătateaMea portal.

## Q3. Which narrow wedges grew into platforms in CEE/Nordics?

### Takeaway
The clearest wedge-to-platform paths all start with **one painful, frequent workflow for a provider**: booking (Docplanner), clinical documentation (Tandem), ECG reading (Cardiomatics, Medicalgorithmics), triage (Platform24, Infermedica) and oncology symptom monitoring (Kaiku). Each wedge then expanded **sideways into adjacent workflows of the same buyer**. Docplanner went from directory to booking marketplace, practice SaaS and the Noa AI suite (notes, evidence, voice booking agent). Tandem went from scribe to coding and decision support (MDR IIa) and an "AI-native operating system for clinical workflows". The broad-portfolio approach (Comarch) shrank.

## Q4. What can Romania learn from Estonia, Finland and Sweden about sequencing interoperability and the role of private software vendors?

### Takeaway
The three systems used different sequences, but each paired **a shared exchange layer with a legal mandate and a vendor-facing rulebook**:
- **Estonia** built the generic data-exchange layer first (X-Road, 2001, open-source). It then legally required **all** providers to send records to a central health information system and reached ~99% e-prescriptions. It is now renewing the ageing central system (UpTIS).
- **Finland** made connection to **Kanta** **legally mandatory for private providers** with electronic records, with no transition period for health care. It created an explicit **certification market**: THL profiles, free Kela joint testing, paid Traficom-accredited security audits, Valvira registry, certificates valid up to 3 years. It also allowed **"joint connection" intermediaries** for chains and franchises.
- **Sweden** uses a **region-owned national operator (Inera/1177)** that connects "hundreds" of underlying systems in a standardised way and procures private platforms (Platform24, Visiba). Its Platform24 episode shows the patient-safety and MDR risk of scaling triage without certification.

For Romania, the lesson is that **the private-vendor opportunity appears when the state publishes stable interfaces plus certification rules and mandates connection**. Romania's PIAS / e-SănătateaMea timeline (Dec 2025 → Aug 2026 → Q4 2026 mandate) is the moment to watch.

### Cited Findings

**Estonia**
- X-Road is a data-exchange layer in which systems do not exchange data directly. It has run **since 2001** and is **MIT-licensed, free**. [Established] — [e-Estonia X-Road](https://e-estonia.com/service/interoperability-services/)
- An EU-commissioned overview of Estonian EHR law (2016) says **all officially recognised healthcare providers are legally required to upload patient EHR data** to the national system, which sits alongside providers' own systems rather than replacing them. [Established, dated 2016] — [European Commission, laws_estonia_en_0.pdf](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/laws_estonia_en_0.pdf)
- A 2023 TEHIK presentation describes **integration through a central system**, an **opt-out** model in which patients can restrict which doctors see their data, and a **patient portal showing who accessed their data**. [Established] — [Linna, eHealth and HL7 in Estonia, Mar 2023 (PDF)](https://collab.hl7.at/download/attachments/69468631/20230315-0920_Linna_eHealth_and_HL7_inEstonia_Austria.pdf?api=v2)
- Estonia's Health Board Director General (Sept 2024): **99% of prescriptions handled online**, e-prescriptions **usable in pharmacies in other EU countries**. Lessons cited: "shared platforms are quicker" and the value of **public-private partnership**. [Strong emerging] — [Birgit Lao, PSN Toronto, 26 Sep 2024 (PDF)](https://cdn.publicsectornetwork.com/insight/pdfs/Birgit_Lao_PSN_Toronto_26092024_Lao_send_522142.pdf); [Public Sector Network interview](https://www.publicsectornetwork.com/insight/lets-chat-lessons-from-estonias-health-revolution)
- **TEHIK's UpTIS project:** the current health information system "has started to limit the development of e-health in Estonia". UpTIS aims at a new, more flexible data-exchange platform; it was in planning and technical renewal, with no date given. [Strong emerging] — [TEHIK UpTIS](https://www.tehik.ee/en/uptis)
- A 2017 TalTech thesis found X-Road was **opened to private entities only "a few years" earlier**, and **few private entities had joined**. [Established, dated] — [Paide 2017, TalTech (PDF)](https://digikogu.taltech.ee/en/Download/d8889a0b-4d26-41be-ae82-ee5463b91890)
- A search summary says Estonia's compulsory provider liability insurance took effect on 1 Nov 2024. This is not an IT rule but adds to the regulatory load. The original source page was unclear and could not be fetched. [Plausible] — [Wikipedia: Healthcare in Estonia (search result; unverified)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Estonia)

**Finland (Kanta)**
- Under the **Client Data Act**, private social and health care providers **must join Kanta if they use an electronic patient information system**. For health care there are **no transitional periods**, and the **Prescription service is mandatory** for prescribers and pharmacies under the Act on Electronic Prescriptions. [Established] — [Kanta: preparatory tasks and joining](https://kanta.fi/en/professionals/preparatory-tasks-and-joining); [Kanta: joining](https://www.kanta.fi/professionals/preparatory-tasks)
- **Connection models:** *direct* (any public or private provider) or *joint*. In the joint model, a provider connected directly connects others as users, for example **franchising chains or groups**. [Established] — [Kanta connection models](https://www.kanta.fi/en/ammattilaiset/liittymismallit)
- **Vendor certification chain:**
  1. **THL** sets minimum requirements via national profiles (regulations 4/2024, 5/2024).
  2. **Kela** runs **joint testing (free of charge)**, required for class **A2/A3** systems and class A wellbeing apps.
  3. A **Traficom-approved inspection body** performs a **paid security assessment**, paid by the system supplier, and issues a certificate **valid up to 3 years**.
  4. **Valvira** registers the system in a public database. A deployment test at each customer follows.
  
  Access points must use Valvira-approved A1/A2/A3 systems. [Established] — [Kanta certification](https://www.kanta.fi/en/system-developers/certification-and-key-requirements); [Certification parties](https://www.kanta.fi/jarjestelmakehittajat/sertifioinnin-osapuolet-ja-vastuut); [From testing to production](https://kanta.fi/en/system-developers/from-testing-to-production); [Kanta: five Q&A on certification](https://www.kanta.fi/en/ajankohtaista/matka-sertifioiduksi-jarjestelmaksi-viisi-kysymysta-ja-vastausta-tietojarjestelmien-sertifiointiprosessista)
- Kanta states that **all public healthcare organisations and a large percentage of private providers** have joined. [Strong emerging] — [Kanta: data security](https://www.kanta.fi/en/ajankohtaista/the-kanta-services-meet-high-standards-for-data-security)

**Sweden (Inera / 1177)**
- **Inera is owned by the regions, municipalities and SKR** and publicly financed. It describes itself as offering data exchange in a **technology- and competition-neutral** way. **Hundreds of underlying systems connect to 1177 in a standardised way**, and 1177.se had **15–20 million visits per month in 2025**. [Established] — [Inera strategy 2026–2028 (PDF)](https://www.inera.se/globalassets/inera/media/dokument/om-inera/ineras-uppdrag/ineras-verksamhetsinriktning-2026-2028.pdf)
- **Each region decides its own digital care flow.** Some use Inera's 1177 Direkt (delivered by Platform24), others procured or built their own apps. [Established] — [SVT](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/nyhetstecken/olika-vagar-for-att-kontakta-varden-beroende-pa-var-du-bor-regionerna-bestammer); [1177 accessibility](https://www.1177.se/om-1177/1177.se/tillganglighet-pa-1177/sa-tillganglig-ar-1177-direkt/)
- Private providers in Swedish urban regions are largely **tax-funded under regional agreements**. [Strong emerging] — [TalTech news](https://taltech.ee/en/news/does-estonia-have-anything-learn-swedish-healthcare-and-e-health)
- Platform24 safety and registration issues (see Q3). [Strong emerging] — [SVT Skåne](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/lokalt/skane/svt-avslojar-hemlig-rapport-underkanner-1177-s-digitala-losning-platform24)

**Romania (current state, for sequencing comparison)**
- PIAS modernisation includes a public booking platform: first version due **Dec 2025**, full version **Aug 2026** (2025 article; completion not confirmed). **e-SănătateaMea**, which lets patients see prescriptions, referrals and history and book online, is **mandatory for CNAS-contracted providers from Q4 2026**. Patient groups want phone booking kept. [Strong emerging] — [Playtech.ro](https://playtech.ro/2025/digitalizarea-serviciilor-medicale-de-stat-in-romania-cand-te-vei-putea-programa-pe-internet-pentru-vizita-la-medic/); [Digi24](https://www.digi24.ro/stiri/actualitate/social/romanii-ar-putea-consulta-online-retetele-trimiterile-si-istoricul-medical-cum-va-functiona-platforma-e-sanatateamea-din-septembrie-3906571); [medic24](https://medic24.ro/portalul-esanatateamea-ajunge-la-promulgare-programari-online-din-trimestrul-iv/)
- AtlasIntel (Mar 2026): 32.6% had booked online in the public system. AtlasIntel (Jun 2026): ~88% would choose online booking for state hospitals. [Strong emerging, polls] — [StartupCafe](https://startupcafe.ro/romanii-si-serviciile-digitale-de-stat-plata-taxelor-pe-primul-loc-ce-vor-ei-in-2026-studiu-96599); [Go4it](https://www.go4it.ro/content/internet/romanii-vor-mai-putine-ghisee-si-mai-multe-servicii-online-ce-arata-noul-studiu-atlasintel-19277915)
- MKOR (2026): **32% of Romanians would feel betrayed if explanations of their medical tests were AI-generated**. This is a trust constraint on patient-facing AI. [Strong emerging] — [StartupCafe](https://startupcafe.ro/jumatate-romani-nu-recunosc-text-inteligenta-artificiala-studiu-107187)

### Inferences
- **[Established, across all three]** **Mandate + interface + certification** is what turned national systems into a market for private vendors. Finland is the clearest: the law forces private providers to connect, and the certification chain (free state testing, paid private audit, public registry) creates demand for certified systems and **intermediary/"joint connection" services**. Estonia shows that a mandate plus a central record yields near-universal e-prescription. Its UpTIS renewal also shows that a first-generation central system becomes a bottleneck after ~15 years.
- **[Strong emerging]** Private uptake is **not automatic** when participation is voluntary (Estonia's X-Road private uptake was slow by 2017). **Romania's Q4 2026 e-SănătateaMea mandate is the trigger that matters**: providers will need help connecting, reconciling data and training staff.
- **[Plausible]** The Romanian equivalent of Finland's joint-connection intermediaries would be **integration and middleware for small CNAS-contracted clinics, labs and GP practices** between their clinic software (iStoma, icMED, iClinic, in-house Oracle/Excel) and state platforms. It fits the founder's Python/SQL/PL-SQL/APEX/n8n stack. It is **only viable if CNAS/ADR publish stable, documented APIs and allow third-party software**. That is unverified (see Gaps).
- **[Plausible]** Sweden shows that **public procurement of digital-front-door platforms concentrates on 1–3 national-scale vendors** (Platform24 at 18/21 regions; Visiba at >50% of regions). In Romania the analogous buyer (CNAS/MS) builds or procures centrally (PIAS), so a small vendor should not plan to sell the "front door" to the state. Selling **to private providers who must comply** is more realistic.
- **[Strong emerging]** The Platform24 episode is a direct warning. **Any triage, symptom checking or risk prediction for patients is a medical device under MDR**; deploying it unregistered drew regulatory supervision even in Sweden. For "predictive health" ideas, the founder should keep outputs **clinician-facing and administrative**, or budget for MDR conformity, which is far beyond €25k.
- **[Plausible]** Romania could copy Estonia's **patient-visible access log** and Finland's **public registry of certified systems**. Both are cheap trust mechanisms. A Romanian vendor that offers audit trails and GDPR-grade access logging out of the box would be aligned with where regulation is heading (EHDS).

### Gaps
- **Romanian technical rules for vendors were not verified.** Open questions: whether CNAS/SIUI/PIAS/e-SănătateaMea publish **public APIs or interface specifications** for third-party software, whether there is any **software homologation/certification** process, and how **Dosarul Electronic de Sănătate (DES)** uptake has evolved. [BK-unverified] DES has existed for over a decade with low usage, and CNAS reporting has relied on desktop/third-party clinic software exporting to SIUI. Verify with CNAS technical documentation and ADR.
- **EHDS** (European Health Data Space) obligations for EHR-system vendors (conformity, self-declaration, EU-wide formats) and their timeline were not researched this session.
- **Lithuania (ESPBI IS), Latvia (E-veselība), Croatia (CEZIH) and Czech eRecept** were not researched (budget). [BK-unverified leads]:
  - Latvia's E-veselība had a troubled, mandatory rollout around 2017–2018, which is a cautionary analogue for Romania.
  - Croatia's CEZIH (central system, e-prescription/e-referral, ~2011) and Czech eRecept (mandatory ~2018) are closer in budget level to Romania.
  - Verify via WHO Europe / European Observatory country profiles.
- Exact **Estonian vendor roles** (Cybernetica for X-Road; Nortal and Helmes for health system components) are [BK-unverified]. Check e-Estonia and TEHIK procurement records.
- **Kanta history dates** (e-prescription mandatory ~2017; Patient Data Repository ~2014) are [BK-unverified] for the precise years.

### Inferences
- **[Plausible]** **Best-fit pattern 1: "mandate-driven integration middleware"** (Finland joint-connection analogue). Help small CNAS-contracted clinics, labs and GP practices in Bihor/Oradea comply with e-SănătateaMea/PIAS from Q4 2026: data sync, booking-slot publishing, status dashboards, reconciliation reports. It is non-device, recurring and local-network-sellable. It depends entirely on unverified API availability (Q4 Gaps).
- **[Plausible]** **Best-fit pattern 2: "Docplanner-lite B2B admin AI"** for private clinics: missed-call recovery, booking confirmations, no-show reduction and Romanian-language visit-note drafting for the doctor to approve. Validated abroad (Noa Booking, Noa Notes, Tandem) and locally (MedOcean, receptie-clinica.ai price points of €150/month + usage). Competition is rising, so differentiation would have to come from **integration with Romanian clinic software (iStoma, icMED, iClinic)** and **lab workflows** (Synevo-style independent labs in Bihor).
- **[Plausible]** **Best-fit pattern 3: "Kaiku-style narrow module"** for a preventive/predictive workflow that stays clinician-facing. Examples: recall lists for chronic patients overdue for tests, built from the clinic's own data with SQL rules, not ML diagnosis. Rules-based reminders and lists are generally outside MDR, but **any patient-specific risk score or diagnosis suggestion likely falls under MDR Rule 11 (Class IIa+)** [Strong emerging, by analogy with Tandem/Platform24].
- **[Strong emerging]** **Patterns to avoid at this scale:** a consumer marketplace (Docplanner needed €100M+ and still skipped Romania), regulated AI diagnostics (Cardiomatics, Medicalgorithmics, Infermedica), B2G platforms (Platform24, Visiba) and DiGA-style therapeutics (no Romanian reimbursement route found).
- **[Speculative]** Being in **Bihor/Oradea** (near Hungary) could make a Hungarian-language or cross-border clinic offering a niche. No evidence was gathered on Hungarian clinic-software gaps.
