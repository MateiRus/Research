# Consumer and B2B2C preventive/predictive health companies: evidence table, business models, failures (as of 9 Oct 2026)

**Method note for the report writer.** 30 web searches were completed on 9 Oct 2026; 6 more were refused after the shared search budget ran out. Direct page fetches were blocked by the egress proxy (Fierce Healthcare, Sifted, Neko Health, Withings media CDN, Renaissance Capital, valueaddvc and hitconsultant all returned 403). As a result, **almost every fact below comes from search-result snippets** that summarise the cited URL. I did not read those pages directly. The shared web-search budget then ran out, so some planned checks were not done. They are listed under Gaps: Function's clinical criticism, current Thriva status, ZOE trial evidence, physician-network vendors, Prenuvo in 2026, and EU direct-to-consumer (DTC) testing law. Evidence labels used: **[Established]** means a filing, regulator document or audited/S-1 figure. **[Strong emerging]** means a company press release or several consistent press reports. **[Plausible but unproven]** means a CEO claim, a single outlet or a third-party estimate. **[Speculative]** means my own inference.

## Objective: Company-by-company evidence table (31 entities; 20 with substantive data)

### Takeaway
Consumers do pay for preventive products at scale in two forms: wearables with a subscription (Oura, Whoop) and low-priced lab or scan memberships (Function, Neko). The profitable or near-profitable players are hardware-plus-subscription companies (Oura, Whoop, Withings, Ultrahuman) and one high-ticket imaging provider (Prenuvo, by its CEO's claim only). Lab-testing memberships are well funded but show no disclosed profit, and their prices are falling fast. The failures share capital-heavy physical delivery (Forward), dependence on reimbursement (Pear), money-losing risk contracts plus a failed merger (Babylon), or small DTC volumes in Europe (Aware). For a Romanian solo founder, the transferable part is the B2B software layer behind these companies. That layer covers lab integration, structuring of results, physician review queues, longitudinal dashboards and recall/rebooking. The consumer brand itself does not transfer.

#### C. Imaging / scan-based preventive check-ups

**10. Neko Health (Sweden/UK/US)**
- *Product:* a 60-minute body scan run on in-house hardware (skin imaging, cardiovascular and metabolic measures, bloods) with a doctor consultation included. *Payer:* consumer. *Pricing:* **£299 in the UK** and **$499 at the New York (SoHo, "Lafayette") clinic** ([Neko NYC page](https://www.nekohealth.com/us/en/locations/nyc/lafayette); [Neko Series B release](https://www.nekohealth.com/gb/en/neko-health-raises-260m-series-b)) [Strong emerging].
- *Funding:* $260M Series B (Jan 2025, Lightspeed lead) at ~$1.8B ([Neko](https://www.nekohealth.com/gb/en/neko-health-raises-260m-series-b); [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/neko-health-series-b-funding/)). **$700M Series C on 15 Jul 2026** (Lightspeed lead, O.G. co-lead) at a reported **~$7B valuation** from people familiar with the deal, not from Neko ([Radiology Business](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/healthcare-management/healthcare-economics/whole-body-imaging-firm-neko-health-raises-700m); [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/07/15/neko-health-raises-700-million-series-c/); [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/venture-capital/neko-health-700-million-series-c-7-billion-valuation-vc-thesis)) [Strong emerging].
- *Traction:* in Jan 2025 Neko had done 10,000 scans with a waitlist of >100k. By the Series C, **>100,000 scans had been completed** in the UK and Sweden and 350k+ people were on the waitlist or registered. **~75% of members book and prepay their next annual scan before leaving** ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/neko-health-raises-700-million-series-c-ahead-of-u-s-launch/)) [Strong emerging: company claim, the strongest retention signal outside Oura].
- *Clinical data (company-published, uncontrolled):* the Stockholm 2024 data covers 4,362 scans. Of these, 81.3% needed no follow-up and 18.7% were referred. Results among those referred: 1.2% had a previously unknown life-threatening condition, 4.0% a significant condition and 1.2% a reversible condition. 4.9% were cleared after follow-up tests, and 6.7% went to their own provider or did not attend. Neko owns **Atrium, a primary-care clinic inside the Swedish public system**, and pays for referrals there. The report states it is "not a scientific study with a control group" ([Neko Data Story Year Two](https://www.nekohealth.com/neko-data-story-year-two)). In year one (2023), 6.6% had a moderate-to-severe condition ([Year One](https://www.nekohealth.com/neko-data-story-year-one)).

**11. Prenuvo (US/Canada): whole-body MRI**
- *Pricing:* the flagship full-body MRI costs $2,500. Since early 2025 there is a **$3,999 bundle** (scan + blood test + neuro + body composition) ([NBC/CNBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/prenuvo-adds-new-health-tests-to-flagship-full-body-scan-raises-120-million-in-fresh-funding/3753868/)) [Strong emerging].
- *Funding/traction:* a $120M Series B (closed 2024, announced Feb 2025), co-led by Forerunner, Left Lane and Felicis ([Axios Pro](https://www.axios.com/pro/health-tech-deals/2025/02/13/prenuvo-la-announces-previous-120m-raise-new-fat-tests)). The CEO also points to sales to **self-insured employers**.
- *Evidence/criticism:* a 2019 review of 12 studies (>5,000 asymptomatic people) found incidental findings common and a 16% false-positive prevalence across 6 studies. A 2020 study found 95% of asymptomatic people had some "abnormal" finding, of which 1.8% were cancer ([AOL/Fortune summary](https://www.aol.com/articles/often-full-body-mris-actually-130019328.html); [Fortune Well](https://www.fortune.com/well/article/prenuvo-full-body-mri-scan-benefits-drawbacks)) [Established for the cited studies]. The ACR advises against WB-MRI for average-risk people, and the American College of Preventive Medicine has advised against these scans ([Fortune Well](https://www.fortune.com/well/article/prenuvo-full-body-mri-scan-benefits-drawbacks); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/health-tech-deals/2025/02/13/prenuvo-la-announces-previous-120m-raise-new-fat-tests)). Prenuvo's own Polaris study followed 1,011 patients; 41 were biopsied, and more than half of those had cancer. The rebuttal letter defending the scans is written by employees who hold equity ([Prenuvo letter](https://prenuvo.com/research/letter-to-the-editor-evaluating-whole-body-mri-screening-on-its-own-merits)). The FDA-cleared brain tools were cleared via **manufacturer partners**, not Prenuvo ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/health-tech-deals/2025/02/13/prenuvo-la-announces-previous-120m-raise-new-fat-tests)).

## Key Question 1: Do consumers pay sustainably for preventive testing, wearables and PHR, and what are the churn/retention signals?

### Takeaway
Yes, for wearables with a subscription. Oura shows 5.0M paid members with ~85% 12-month retention [Established], and Whoop reports 2.5M+ members [Strong emerging]. Possibly yes for low-priced annual scans and lab memberships: Neko reports a ~75% pre-booking rate, and Function's repeat financing points the same way, but no churn figures are disclosed. Evidence is weak or negative for high-touch, test-heavy nutrition and CGM programmes (ZOE, Levels) and for OTC CGM demand (Stelo below forecasts). Pure personal health record (PHR) products had no standalone revenue evidence in this research.

### Cited Findings
- **Oura:** 5.0M paid members (+100% YoY) with **12-month paid-member retention ≈85%**. Membership revenue was $240.5M for the 9 months to Jun 2026, about 20% of revenue ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/121786/smart-ring-maker-oura-sets-terms-for-2-1-billion-ipo); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91602373/oura-ring-ipo-stock-listing-date-nears-see-whats-in-sec-filing)) [Established].
- **Whoop:** 2.5M+ members, with subscriptions and bookings +103% in 2025 (company-reported) ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/whoop-raises-575-million-10-204207038.html)) [Strong emerging].
- **Neko:** ~75% of members book and prepay their next annual scan before leaving the clinic ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/neko-health-raises-700-million-series-c-ahead-of-u-s-launch/)) [Strong emerging, company claim]. The Stockholm 2024 numbers included 1,477 follow-up scans out of 4,362 ([Neko Year Two](https://www.nekohealth.com/neko-data-story-year-two)).
- **Function:** no churn has been disclosed. The bear case is "test once, learn baseline, don't renew", which is a hypothesis without data ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/function-health-at-100m-year/); [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/digital-health-function-health-overvalued)).
- **ZOE:** the CEO said 2024 growth forecasts "were wrong" and costs were cut ~20% ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/zoe-healthtech-cost-cutting-news)). Gross margin was only 28% in FY23 ([The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/health/can-zoe-app-trim-enough-of-its-own-fat-to-fight-fit-again/690316.article)). The company has since moved toward a cheaper app-only product plus retail ([Simplify](https://simplify.jobs/c/ZOE), low reliability).
- **Levels:** repricing put pre-Apr 2026 members on legacy plans and moved new members to a cheaper app tier with optional sensors ([Levels support](https://support.levels.com/article/764-levels-classic-core-and-complete-plans); [Body Science Review](https://bodysciencereview.com/blog/levels-cgm-continuous-glucose-monitor-review/)). It also launched a B2B channel, Levels Pro ([Levels Pro](https://www.levels.com/pro/compare/dariohealth)).
- **OTC CGM:** Stelo made ~$130M in 2025 versus BTIG's 2024 forecast of $190M ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/dexcom-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-02-12/); [MDDI](https://www.mddionline.com/diabetes/cgm-coming-soon-to-your-online-shopping-cart)) [Established actual; forecast from search snippet].
- **Price sensitivity:** Superpower offers $49/yr through IM8 ([AOL/Prenetics](https://lite.aol.com/sports/other/story/0022/20260324/9677608.htm)), and Function cut its price to $365 ([MedCity](https://medcitynews.com/2025/11/function-health-startup-testing-imaging/)).
- **Romania:** fewer than half of Romanians visit a doctor preventively, and 3 in 10 had no routine check-up in 2025 ([Agerpres / Regina Maria study](https://agerpres.ro/ots/studiu-trei-din-zece-romani-nu-au-fost-in-2025-la-niciun-control-de-rutina-alti-doi-din-zece-au-fost--660254)) [Strong emerging, company-commissioned].
- **Europe DTC blood testing:** Aware had ~10k active customers when its assets were sold ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/aeon-buys-aware-health-assets-in-eur-expansion-push)).

### Inferences
- **[Strong emerging]** Retention is highest when the product creates a **recurring physical or behavioural loop**: a ring worn daily, or an annual scan pre-booked at checkout. It is weakest when the value is a one-time insight, as with a CGM month or a nutrition test. For a software supplier, **recall and rebooking automation** and **longitudinal trend display** directly address the main economic weakness of one-off tests.
- **[Plausible but unproven]** Romanian consumers' low uptake of routine check-ups suggests that, as in the rest of the EU, payers will mostly be employers and insurers rather than individuals paying US-style $365 memberships. This is backed by the NN/Regina Maria bundle and the Irish Life €50 benefit.
- **[Speculative]** "Pay sustainably" in Europe more likely means €50–€300 per year per member paid by employers or insurers, plus individual top-ups, than €365+ paid directly by consumers.

### Gaps
- No disclosed churn for Function, Superpower, Whoop, Neko (beyond prebooking), ZOE or Levels.
- No evidence that a standalone consumer PHR product is paid for; this was not specifically searched.
- No independent cohort data, for example on retention by price point.

## Key Question 2: Which companies are profitable or near-profitable, which failed, and why?

### Takeaway
Verified or claimed profitability clusters in **hardware+subscription wearables**: Oura (S-1 net income), Ultrahuman (FY25 net profit), Whoop (cash-flow positive, company claim) and Withings (profitable FY25, company claim). Two outside that group claim profit through their CEOs: Prenuvo, a high-ticket scan provider, and Ada, which sells B2B licences. The failures are explained by four mechanisms: (1) capital-heavy physical delivery with no proven demand (Forward), (2) reimbursement that never came (Pear), (3) loss-making risk-bearing care plus a failed financing (Babylon), and (4) sub-scale DTC volumes in Europe (Aware; ZOE's distress). Halo shows that even Big Tech exits when a commoditised wearable lacks differentiation.

### Cited Findings
- **Oura:** net income of $60.8M for the 9 months to 30 Jun 2026, alongside a disclosed "history of operating losses" ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/121786/smart-ring-maker-oura-sets-terms-for-2-1-billion-ipo)) [Established].

### Inferences
- **[Strong emerging]** The profitable players earn margin on **hardware plus recurring software**, and the clinical review is either light (Oura, Whoop) or packaged as a fixed-cost add-on (Withings' 4 cardiologist reviews per year). Models whose cost of goods includes **labs, phlebotomy, MRI time or clinicians per member** (Function, Neko, Prenuvo, ZOE, Forward) need high volume or high prices. Only Prenuvo claims profit, and it does so at $2,500+ per scan.
- **[Strong emerging]** In both Babylon and Aware, the acquirer bought **operational assets**: patient panels, lab integrations, draw networks and teams. This suggests the durable value is in the operational and software infrastructure, not the consumer brand.
- **[Plausible but unproven]** Pear shows that in DTx and prevention, a regulatory authorisation does not guarantee anyone will pay. The same risk applies in Romania, where CNAS (the national health insurance fund) does not reimburse digital preventive tools (not verified this session).

### Gaps
- No audited profitability figures for Whoop, Withings, Prenuvo or Ada.
- Babylon's US risk-based contracts as the main loss driver is background knowledge only and was not re-verified this session.
- Halo's subscriber numbers were never public.

## Key Question 3: What software infrastructure do these companies depend on that could be sold as B2B, and what could a small Romanian company supply?

### Takeaway
Every consumer brand here depends on a **stack of five recurring components**:
- (a) lab network and ordering/results integration (Quest, Junction, Aware's "live lab integrations")
- (b) sample collection logistics (Quest/PSCs, Getlabs, 45 draw sites)
- (c) a **clinician review workflow** (Withings' cardiologist reviews within 24 h, Superpower's "medical team", Neko's doctor consultation and Atrium referrals, Irish Life's clinician-reviewed report)
- (d) **longitudinal data and AI interpretation** (Function's "medical intelligence" model, health scores and biological age, Levels' "unlimited uploads of past bloodwork")
- (e) **recall, rebooking and follow-up** (Neko's 75% prepay, follow-up scans, referral tracking)

Parts (c), (d) and (e) are software-only. A solo Romanian founder could build them for Romanian clinics, lab networks, employers or insurers without owning labs or devices [Plausible but unproven].

### Cited Findings
- **Junction:** sells a lab-testing API to digital-health companies (ordering, collection, results through CLIA/CAP labs) and raised $18M in 2025. This shows lab integration is a fundable standalone B2B layer ([Junction docs](https://docs.junction.com/lab/overview/introduction); [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/junction/funding-rounds/series-a-2025-03)) [Established].
- **Function:** relies on Quest's ~2,200 sites ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/function-health-ezra-acquired-and-499-full-body-mri-scan-introduced)), bought Getlabs for at-home draws ([MobiHealthNews snippet](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/function-health-secures-450m-growth-financing)) and built an AI interpretation model ([Fierce](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/function-health-lands-298m-series-b-rolls-out-medical-intelligence-ai-model-health-data)).
- **Ezra:** asset-light. It supplied scanning protocol, AI and workflow to third-party imaging centres rather than owning MRIs ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/full-body-mri-scanning-company-ezra-gets-acquired-by-function-health)) [Strong emerging]. This is a "software on someone else's scanner" model; Ezra's founder is Romanian ([Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/function-health-acquires-ezra-may-2025)).
- **Withings:** Cardio Check-Up is a productised physician review queue, with cardiologist feedback within 24 h and 4 reviews included in a €99.95/yr subscription ([Withings PR](https://media.withings.com/press/press-releases/bpm-vision-cardio-check-up-ces/CES-2025_Withings_BPM-Vision-Cardio-Check-Up_PR_Final.pdf)) [Strong emerging].
- **Neko:** owns its referral pathway (the Atrium primary-care clinic) and runs in-clinic follow-up testing to "minimise false positives". This is a referral-tracking workflow with measured outcomes: the Year Two categories cover 815 referred members ([Neko Year Two](https://www.nekohealth.com/neko-data-story-year-two)).
- **Aeon's acquisition of Aware:** valued the technology platform, live lab integrations, draw network and customer relationships ([IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/aeon-buys-aware-health-assets-in-eur-expansion-push)).
- **Levels:** Classic includes "unlimited uploads of past bloodwork", which implies parsing externally produced lab reports ([Levels support](https://support.levels.com/article/764-levels-classic-core-and-complete-plans)).
- **Ada:** shows that consumer-facing AI triage is sold B2B as a white-label or embedded module to insurers (Groupe Mutuel, Santéclair) and health systems (Jefferson, Epic App Orchard) ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/tag/ada-health/)).
- **Romanian precedents:** Medicai is a cloud imaging collaboration SaaS with 70 customers, most in Romania ([Business Forum](https://www.businessforum.ro/industry/20250807/medicai-a-romanian-health-tech-startup-expands-in-the-us-2164)). SanoPass, a prevention access platform, was acquired by MedLife ([The Diplomat](https://www.thediplomat.ro/?p=21719)). Both are evidence of buyers and of an acquisition path in Romania.
- **Romanian buyers:** the private groups are large (MedLife ~3.17bn lei, Regina Maria ~3.0bn lei, Medicover ~1.52bn lei in 2025) and already sell check-up packages and subscriptions ([Economedia](https://economedia.ro/piata-serviciilor-medicale-private-continua-sa-creasca.html); [Regina Maria PDF](https://www.reginamaria.ro/sites/default/files/2025-03/1DayCheckUpfemei-craiova.pdf)).

### Inferences: candidate software layers for a Romanian solo founder (€25k, 10–12 h/week)
1. **Lab-report structuring + longitudinal dashboard.** Parse PDF and HL7 results from Romanian labs (Synevo, Bioclinica, MedLife and Regina Maria labs) into structured, unit-normalised time series, then show per-patient trends to clinics and occupational-health providers. This is an equivalent of Levels' bloodwork uploads and Function's dashboard, sold to small clinics and medicina-muncii (occupational health) firms. Fits LLM, Oracle APEX and n8n skills. **[Plausible but unproven]**
2. **Physician review queue ("results inbox").** An LLM drafts a plain-language summary and flags out-of-range or trending values; a physician approves or edits; the system keeps an audit trail and SLA timers. This mirrors Withings' 24 h cardiologist review and Irish Life's clinician-reviewed report, sold per review or per seat to check-up providers and insurers' preventive programmes. **Regulatory caution:** if the software itself makes diagnostic or prognostic claims, it is likely medical-device software under EU MDR, and IVD-related software may fall under IVDR. Positioning it as workflow and documentation with the clinician deciding lowers this risk (background knowledge, not verified this session). **[Plausible but unproven]**
3. **Recall, rebooking and follow-up tracking.** Annual check-up recall, an abnormal-result follow-up loop and referral closure tracking ("did the patient attend?"). Neko's 75% prepay and its explicit tracking of no-show referrals show this is a core economic lever. It suits n8n automation over SMS, WhatsApp and email. **[Plausible but unproven]**
4. **Employer and insurer preventive-programme reporting.** Aggregated, anonymised dashboards for employers buying check-up packages, or for insurers like NN that bundle lab packages. The B2B2C route is shown by Irish Life/Inuvi and Ada/Groupe Mutuel. **[Speculative]**
5. **Avoid** building a consumer lab-membership brand, which requires labs, phlebotomy and marketing spend; Aware failed after raising double-digit € millions. Also avoid hardware and scans: Neko raised at least $960M in its Series B and C. **[Strong emerging]**

### Gaps
- Physician-network-as-a-service vendors (e.g., Wheel, OpenLoop) could not be researched because the search budget was exhausted. These are the US analogue of option 2.
- No data on what Romanian clinics or labs currently use for result delivery, or whether they would pay for a review queue. This needs customer interviews.
- Whether Romanian labs expose HL7/FHIR or only PDF/portal output was not verified.

## Key Question 4: Which models depend on US-specific factors that don't transfer to Romania or the EU?

### Takeaway
Several of the best-funded US models rest on US-specific conditions:
- national CLIA lab chains with thousands of draw sites (Quest, ~2,000–2,200 sites)
- state-by-state DTC testing permissions (NY/NJ restrictions)
- HSA/FSA tax-advantaged spending (Whoop since Nov 2025, OTC CGMs)
- self-insured employers buying scans (Prenuvo)
- FDA enforcement latitude for "wellness" claims (Whoop's warning letter was closed after the 2026 guidance update)

In the EU and Romania, payers, medical-device law (MDR/IVDR) and GDPR shape the model differently. The transferable parts are **subscription wearables** (sold EU-wide at euro prices), **per-scan clinics with annual rebooking** (Neko in Sweden and the UK), and **insurer or employer-paid benefits** (Irish Life, NN/Regina Maria, Ada).

### Cited Findings
- **US lab networks:** Function and Superpower depend on Quest's national draw network (~2,200 and 2,000+ sites) ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/function-health-ezra-acquired-and-499-full-body-mri-scan-introduced); [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/superpower-launches-199-membership-to-help-democratize-concierge-level-healthcare-nationwide/)) [Strong emerging].
- **State-level DTC rules:** Quest and LabCorp restrictions blocked DTC lab ordering in NY/NJ until Junction's BioReference partnership (Oct 2024). Superpower charges $399 in NY/NJ versus $199 elsewhere ([Junction](https://www.junction.com/post/lab-testing-new-york-new-jersey); [Athletech](https://athletechnews.com/superpower-199-membership-preventive-care/)) [Established].
- **HSA/FSA:** Whoop memberships and Advanced Labs became HSA/FSA-eligible on 13 Nov 2025 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/whoop-at-1b-year-growing-103-yoy/)) [Plausible but unproven].
- **FDA regime:** OTC CGMs (Stelo, Lingo) are FDA-cleared OTC products ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/abbott-dexcom-over-the-counter-cgm-launch/719928/)). Whoop's wellness-vs-device dispute was resolved under the FDA's updated wellness guidance ([FDA letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/whoop-inc-709755-07142025); [Design News](https://www.designnews.com/medical/fda-backs-down-on-whoop-blood-pressure-monitoring-dispute)) [Established].
- **Self-insured employers:** Prenuvo's CEO cites them as the channel for scans ([CNBC via NBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/prenuvo-adds-new-health-tests-to-flagship-full-body-scan-raises-120-million-in-fresh-funding/3753868/)).
- **US reimbursement:** Pear failed on US reimbursement; Medicare did not cover PDTs ([In Vivo](https://invivo.pharmaintelligence.informa.com/IV147722/Pear-Bankruptcy-Filing-Highlights-Reimbursement-Barriers-for-Digital-Therapeutics)). Germany's DiGA is the EU counter-example, but it was not researched here.
- **EU transfer cases:**
  - Whoop sells EU tiers at €199/€264/€399 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250508546933/en/WHOOP-Unveils-WHOOP-5.0-and-WHOOP-MG-Powerful-New-Devices-with-Breakthrough-Health-and-Longevity-Features)).
  - Withings+ costs €99.95/yr and its devices carry CE-MDR certification ([Withings PR](https://media.withings.com/press/press-releases/bpm-vision-cardio-check-up-ces/CES-2025_Withings_BPM-Vision-Cardio-Check-Up_PR_Final.pdf); [Withings press](https://www.withings.com/us/en/press)).
  - Neko operates at £299 in the UK and in Sweden, with its own clinic inside the Swedish public system ([Neko Year Two](https://www.nekohealth.com/neko-data-story-year-two)).
  - Irish Life Health offers a €50 insurer benefit ([Irish Life](https://www.irishlife.ie/health-insurance/health-benefits/essential-health-check/)).
  - Ada is distributed via Swiss and French insurers ([MedCity](https://medcitynews.com/tag/ada-health/)).
- **Germany:** Aware's DTC blood testing through dm drugstores went insolvent ([Apotheke Adhoc](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/markt/blutanalysen-dm-partner-ist-pleite/print.html)).
- **Romania:** insurer-funded preventive lab packages already exist (NN at Regina Maria) ([NN guide](https://www.nn.ro/sites/default/files/2025-03/GhidRM_EnergieSiVitalitate_mar2025.pdf)). Large private groups dominate delivery ([Economedia](https://economedia.ro/piata-serviciilor-medicale-private-continua-sa-creasca.html)).

### Inferences
- **[Strong emerging]** The **US DTC lab-membership model (Function, Superpower) does not transfer directly** to Romania or the EU. It relies on (i) national lab chains willing to serve third-party brands at scale, (ii) US consumer willingness to pay $199–$499 per year out of pocket, aided by HSA/FSA, and (iii) US marketing scale. In Romania the labs (Synevo/Medicover, MedLife, Regina Maria, Bioclinica) **are themselves the consumer brands**. A newcomer would be reselling a competitor's tests. The workable role is supplier to those groups or to independent clinics.
- **[Plausible but unproven]** The **software layers** in Key Question 3 (structuring, review queue, recall, employer reporting) are mostly **jurisdiction-neutral**. EU-specific additions would be GDPR Art. 9 health-data processing (DPA and DPIA), EU or Romanian hosting, and a careful MDR/IVDR intended-purpose statement. These are background facts, not re-verified this session.
- **[Speculative]** Software inspired by HSA/FSA eligibility has no Romanian equivalent. Employer-paid medical subscriptions and occupational-health (medicina muncii) obligations are the closest local payer mechanism and deserve validation by other researchers.

### Gaps
- EU and Romanian law on DTC laboratory testing (whether a physician order is required by country, and how IVDR applies to self-tests and to result-interpretation software) could not be researched because the search budget was exhausted. Other researchers or the regulation workstream should verify.
- Romanian tax treatment of employer-paid medical subscriptions (the deductibility cap) was not verified here.
- Germany's DiGA reimbursement route, a counterpoint to Pear, was not researched.
