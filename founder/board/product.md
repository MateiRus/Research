# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The honest parts of the offer are real product work: a written specification, the second instalment held until the site matches it, 90 days of free fixes, clear ownership, and an explicit list of what is not promised. These are touchpoints the buyer sees, and they are designed.
- The two PRO automations start from real moments in a consultant's day: a prospect fills a form and someone must decide whether to spend an hour on them; a client is late with documents and the application stalls. The missing-items list with reminders is the clearest single feature in the brief.
- The radar pilot is scoped tightly: three sources, five profiles, 30 days, a human check before anything goes out.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The AI eligibility pre-check is the riskiest moment in the product and it is unmeasured.** If it wrongly turns away an eligible prospect, the consultant loses a success fee on a project worth tens to hundreds of thousands of euros and never trusts the system again. If it admits everyone, it removes none of the triage pain. No accuracy figure exists, no test set of past requests exists, and it is unclear whether the prospect ever sees the "stated reason". One bad call in the first week ends the subscription.

2. **Three products, one person, 10-12 hours a week.** START, PRO and RADAR are three different experiences with three different support promises, including human-reviewed alerts by the next working day. The brief's own arithmetic leaves 20 hours a month at five clients. Every seam runs through one calendar: exam weeks, trips to Bihor, the day job. The Product lens asks what to cut; nothing has been cut yet.

3. **START is not distinguishable in the buyer's first five minutes.** What justifies 1,200 EUR against a 389 EUR Oradea site (FAQ written the way people search, structured data, a structured lead form) is invisible on a demo. With no portfolio, the owner compares two screenshots and a price. The quality lives where nobody can see it.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Describe, minute by minute, what a consultant sees on the day PRO goes live. What arrives in their inbox? What do they click?
- When the pre-check says "probably ineligible", what exactly happens to the prospect, and to the consultant?
- Run the pre-check on 50 real past requests from one consultant. How many does it get right?
- The document-collection link goes to the consultant's clients, who have never heard of ILLUSTRUS. Whose name is on it, and what does a reminder look like?
- Say the business in one sentence, without the word "and".
- If you could sell only one of the three, which one, and why not the other two?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. One lead product for the first twelve months. The Product lens would pick PRO (or the two automations sold alone), with START as an add-on and RADAR parked until a PRO client asks for it.
2. Before the first sale, a measured pre-check: a test set of real past requests from at least one consultant, a stated accuracy figure, and a defined path for the "ineligible" case that never silently drops a prospect.
3. Five customer interviews that walk through the live-day experience, not the hypotheses.
4. A written coverage plan for alerts and support hours during exams and travel.
