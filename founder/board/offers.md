# Offers lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The buyer has money and is easy to find. Grant consultants earn success fees on projects worth tens to hundreds of thousands of euros, there is a public AFIR list filterable by county, phone calls are legal, and the founder sits inside the referral network (own grant consultant, GAL). Two of the four market tests pass cleanly.
- The PRO automations and RADAR point at the right kind of pain: hours lost triaging ineligible requests, applications blocked by late client documents, calls discovered late. Those are problems a success-fee business will pay to remove, because each one costs them a fee.
- The risk reversal is real: written specification, second instalment withheld until the site matches it, 90 days of free fixes. The two launch slots at 20% off for a case study are genuine scarcity, not manufactured.
- Delivery cost is tiny (about 40 EUR/month infrastructure), so there is room to add value to the stack without hurting margin.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The lead product is a commodity priced above the local ceiling with zero proof.** START is a presentation website at 1,200 EUR in a city where nobody publishes above ~990 EUR and the national median is ~1,000. The differences (interview-written texts, structured data, lead form) are features, not a different outcome. The Offers lens says the first thing to fix is the market and the dream outcome; a brochure site gives a grant consultant no new clients and no faster fee. Perceived likelihood is near zero: no portfolio, no case study, no signed client. The buyer has every reason to say no.
2. **RADAR competes with free.** National portals, a 16,000-subscriber newsletter and the regional PDF bulletin already deliver call information at no cost. The pilot asks 1,100 EUR for 30 days before any proof, on a pain that has not been validated by a single interview. Value here lives only in the matching against client profiles and the founder's checking. Unless that saves the consultant a quantifiable number of hours or catches a call they would have missed, it is a nice-to-have.
3. **The stack cannot be made much bigger without breaking delivery.** About 20 hours a month remain for new builds at five clients, i.e. one PRO every 2–3 months. The 10,000 EUR path needs five PRO sales with no pipeline, so the offer must convert on first contact. Today it does not; it asks the buyer to believe, wait weeks, and pay above market.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Of ten consultants in Bihor, how many will say on the phone that ineligible-request triage or late client documents cost them a fee in the last year? What is the number of hours per week? (Open question; nobody has it.)
- Why is the website the front door rather than the document-collection automation, which attacks a named pain and has no local competitor?
- What would a consultant need to see to believe this works before the first case study exists: a live demo on their own call list? A free 14-day document-collection trial?
- Could the price double and still be the obvious choice? If not, what outcome would have to be added?
- Is the market growing? How many new grant calls and consulting firms per year in Nord-Vest? (Open question.)
- What is the real cost of acquiring one client in hours and travel? (Open question.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Ten customer interviews before any build, confirming at least one of the three pains with a cost in hours or lost fees.
2. Reframe the lead offer around the pain, not the site: PRO (or document collection alone) is the headline; the website is the bonus in the stack.
3. One outcome-linked guarantee on the automation (for example, a stated reduction in document-chasing time or the setup fee is refunded).
4. The two launch clients signed and documented as proof before the full price is charged to anyone.
