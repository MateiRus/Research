# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The founder has done the first thing the Monopoly lens demands: picked a small market. Bihor has roughly 400–470 consulting firms, 60–70% in Oradea, a filterable AFIR list exists, and the buyer is one person, the owner. A one-person firm could plausibly hold a visible share of that.
- RADAR is the only component with a trace of proprietary know-how: matching calls and amendments from three official sources against end-client profiles, with a human check. Nobody in the brief sells that to this buyer. Keeping the engine under licence is the right structure for an asset.
- The PRO automations (eligibility pre-check, document collection) are shaped around the buyer's workflow rather than around "a website", which is where a brand in a narrow segment comes from.
- Distribution is named and legal: phone, in-person, two dated events, referrals via the GAL network.

## What would kill it (most dangerous first)

1. **The paid product is a commodity.** START and PRO are websites plus n8n automations in a market where Oradea agencies publish sites at 100–990 EUR and automation shops charge 300–1,500 EUR per project. Nothing in START is ten times better than the next option; it is better-written. Any local agency can add a "for consultants" page in a week.
2. **The candidate moat is free elsewhere.** Funding-call information comes free from national portals, aggregators with 16,000+ subscribers, and monthly regional bulletins. RADAR's edge must be the profile matching and amendment tracking, not the information. That edge is unproven: zero interviews, so "they learn late" is a hypothesis.
3. **No path from five to a hundred.** Capacity is one PRO every 2–3 months. Five clients meet the grant objective but are not a position anyone defends. Without a low-effort repeatable product (RADAR as a subscription) the firm never escapes hourly delivery, and a Bucharest agency or software vendor can take the segment whenever it bothers.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- What is the secret? What do you know about how grant consultants lose money that a web agency does not, and where did you learn it?
- Of the 400–470 Bihor firms, how many are in the market for a 1,200–2,400 EUR site this year? (Open question; only interviews answer it.)
- What did the first ten phone calls cost in hours, and how many meetings resulted? (Open question: acquisition cost is unknown.)
- If an Oradea agency copies PRO at 990 EUR in month six, what do you still have?
- Why now? Has anything changed in 2026 (programme cycle, MIPE API) that makes RADAR possible today and not two years ago?
- Does the grant count RADAR subscription revenue as "web-related"? That answer decides which product is the business.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Ten interviews with consulting-firm owners before any build, at least six in Bihor, confirming which pain they would pay for and how much.
2. RADAR is sold first or alongside the first PRO, not later. It is the only thing with a chance of becoming proprietary; the website is the door, not the business.
3. Written confirmation from the grant authority on which revenue counts.
4. A defined wedge: Bihor consultants only for twelve months, with a named share target (even "ten of 450") before any Bucharest effort.
5. A copy test: write down what a competitor needs to replicate RADAR and whether a year of accumulated client-profile data is the asset. If it is not, the vote is PASS.
