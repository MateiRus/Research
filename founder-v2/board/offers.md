# Offers lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- B sits in the best market of the three: the pain is forced (a funding-visibility obligation, a reimbursable "presentation website" line), the money is the grant's, buyers sit on public lists (GAL, AFIR, SUN contracting), and Start-Up Nation session 2 opened on 6 Oct 2026, so the pool is growing now.
- Buyer effort is low and specific: a 45-minute interview, 10 phone photos, texts written for them, delivery documents matching the contract wording. That is the part of a stack buyers feel.
- Time to result is short and written down (10 days, 5-10 working days, 24 h response).
- C answers the earlier panel's trust refusal structurally: nothing on the founder's servers, no subscription, a named backup, a measured pilot plus phone reference, and a real guarantee (10 dossiers complete 5 days early or refund). Targeting only 20+ client firms matches the 2-of-20 result.
- Dropping the radar and the consultant-facing website answers "three products, one part-timer" halfway: the losers were cut.

## What would kill it
1. Likelihood is still zero. No client signed, no reference, and both proof devices (free agency task, free 60-day pilot) are unsigned plans. The earlier "no proof" finding is not answered; it is scheduled. Every target rests on two unknowns the brief lists itself: whether any agency accepts, and how many DR36 beneficiaries are within reach.
2. The plan still does not pick a market. Three buyers, three price lines, and a 40% cap that forbids any one offer from winning. This lens wants one offer so good the buyer feels stupid saying no; a hedged portfolio at 10-12 h/week yields three adequate offers. The "three products" finding is only partly answered.
3. B is priced at roughly double the published local rate (800 vs 389 EUR) with no stated reason a beneficiary pays the premium, and no buyer-facing guarantee ("silence in 5 days = accepted" protects the seller). If funding rules make beneficiaries take the lowest of several quotes, B is a commodity at the wrong price. Open question, not asserted.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If a beneficiary must justify the choice to the paying agency, what sentence makes 800 EUR the obvious pick over 389? Could you charge 1,200 and still be chosen?
- What does the C guarantee cost you if the consultant's clients simply do not reply? The kit does not control their behaviour; who bears that refund?
- Which of A, B, C would you keep if only one survived, and what day-30 evidence would make you kill the other two?
- How many contracted DR36 and neighbouring-GAL beneficiaries are reachable? The plan admits the answer changes the targets.
- What does an agency gain from a white-label partner at 150-600 EUR per task that a freelancer at 30-40 EUR/h cannot give?
