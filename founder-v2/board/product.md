# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Offer B ("Project website in 10 days", 800 EUR) is the first thing in either version designed from the customer's moment backward: a 45-minute interview instead of "send us your texts", a photo list, a funding-visibility page in the programme's format, and delivery documents whose invoice wording matches the grant contract. That is what a beneficiary needs to get reimbursed; a 389 EUR template lacks it. Real differentiation, not a price fight.
- Trust, the finding that sank the first buyer panel (14/20 refusals), is answered structurally: everything on client accounts, named backup, measured 60-day pilot, callable reference, refund guarantee, no subscription. The unmeasured AI pre-check and the standalone website are gone. Good cuts.
- The gates and the "no feedback in 5 days = accepted" rule show thought about how delivery ends.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is still three products.** The first board said "three products for one part-time person"; v4 answers with three products for one part-time person, now inside one ecosystem. The identity sentence is a list of three nouns. Worse, the "no source above 40%" rule forbids focus by design: the founder is never allowed to do one thing brilliantly. Three buyers, three onboarding paths, 42 h/month.
2. **The kit (C) hands the experience to the buyer.** The consultant must own domain, hosting, n8n and storage, learn it in 2 hours, then run WhatsApp collection for 20+ clients. The first five minutes are account creation. The refund guarantee ("first 10 dossiers complete 5 days before deadline") depends on the consultant's clients sending files, not on the kit. Every seam is outside the founder's control; the founder's name is on the guarantee.
3. **Offer A promises 24 h response and 5–10 day deadlines from someone with a day job, exams, 10–12 h/week and an unchecked non-compete.** White-label also means the agency owns the client and the credit; the three case studies the plan counts on cannot come from A unless agreed in writing.

Does v4 answer the earlier findings? Trust: largely yes. Commodity: yes for B, no for A. Three products for one person: no. Website nobody wanted: re-aimed at a buyer (beneficiaries, not consultants) the panel never tested; open.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you had to delete two of the three lines today, which stays, and why isn't that the whole plan?
- Walk me through a beneficiary's first five minutes: how they hear of you, what they see, what they sign, what arrives in 10 days. Show one finished site with delivery documents before any sale.
- Who builds the funding-visibility page when the client "provides" nothing usable?
- An agency task lands in exam week, or in a January "unavailable week" the agency's client never agreed to. What happens?
- Who debugs a consultant's n8n at 23:00 before an AFIR deadline?
- How many contracted DR36 beneficiaries are within reach? The plan admits it does not know.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. The day-30 gate picks one product, not a reshuffle of targets. Drop the 40% rule; concentration is the goal, not the risk.
2. Lead with B. Build one complete site, with acceptance report and screenshots, before publishing the price page.
3. Employment contract checked in week 1, before any A work.
4. Kit C only after the pilot shows measured days-to-complete-file; guarantee rewritten around what the kit controls.
5. Any white-label agreement includes the right to a named case study.
